NVIDIA's quarter showed how AI demand is moving through the supply chain

NVIDIA's fiscal third quarter records rapid revenue growth. The results show realised demand while leaving future supply and sales expectations separate.

2024-11-20

NVIDIA reported a sharp increase in quarterly revenue, with data-centre business central to its results. This company ledger separates the completed reporting period from the outlook and from the economics of customers using its equipment.

Results for a defined period

The company announcement dated November 20, 2024 records revenue of $35.1 billion for the quarter ended October 27, up 94% year on year. The fiscal-year label is 2025 even though this quarter ended in calendar 2024.

Reuters reporting, republished by Investing.com, first published on November 20, put Data Center sales at $30.77 billion and the next-quarter revenue outlook at $37.5 billion, plus or minus 2%. The latter is an expectation, not another completed quarter.

What the numbers can tell a customer

A supplier's revenue is not a measurement of the useful work performed by every system its customers buy. For a procurement team, the important distinction is between the equipment transaction and the operating project. A purchase can be necessary for a project without being sufficient to deliver its intended service.

Keep the project milestones separate

  • A contract establishes commercial commitments; it does not establish that a system is operating.
  • Installation needs its own acceptance criteria, rather than an assumption based on a supplier's sales.
  • Service usage and customer receipts belong in an operating review, not in a hardware supplier's revenue total.

These are reading principles, not claims that particular NVIDIA customers have missed a milestone. The reported numbers do not provide a project-by-project assessment of deployment or profitability. Nor should one issuer's sales be labelled the size of the entire AI market: that would require a defined market boundary and evidence covering other participants.

A clear boundary for follow-up

Subsequent comparisons should match the reporting period and the entity being measured. Adding a chip supplier's revenue to a customer's service revenue can count different transactions along the same chain rather than produce a meaningful measure of final demand. Conversely, a slower customer commissioning schedule does not, by itself, contradict a supplier's recorded sale. Each claim needs evidence at the stage where it arises.

The next-quarter outlook should therefore remain in a separate column from actual results. Replacing it with a later outcome would turn a dated announcement into hindsight. This account records the original announcement, not a recommendation about securities or a guarantee of returns from AI investment.
