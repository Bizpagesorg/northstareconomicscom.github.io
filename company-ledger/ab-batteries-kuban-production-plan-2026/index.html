AB-Batteries targets a 2026 start for its Kuban production plant

A January announcement sets out plans for AA and AAA battery production in Kuban. The stated annual capacity applies to full operation, not output already achieved.

2026-01-20

AB-Batteries was planning to commission an alkaline-battery plant in Kuban during 2026, according to regional authorities cited by Interfax on January 20. The project envisaged two lines for AA and AAA batteries, with annual output of 264 million units at full operation. The January report described a plan, not a completed opening.

From a production plan to available orders

The stated annual figure sets the scale of the intended operation. It does not establish how many batteries would be manufactured during the first calendar year. Commissioning partway through a year, testing equipment and establishing a working schedule are different matters from operating for a full year at the stated rate.

For prospective business customers, the practical distinction is between a manufacturing programme and an available supply offer. A production plan identifies the intended product categories. A supply offer would also need quantities, delivery dates and commercial terms for the specific order. The former should not be read as if it already contains the latter.

Separate milestones

  • Commissioning concerns bringing the production facility into operation.
  • Stable output concerns the performance of the working process over time.
  • Delivery commitments concern products allocated to particular customer orders.

These distinctions explain how to read the announcement; they do not add an unpublished company timetable. An annual design figure cannot demonstrate completed sales, a particular retail price or the availability of every pack size. Each of those would require its own product or commercial information.

Product information remains important

AA and AAA identify formats, while a purchasing decision also depends on the specification and intended use. A factory-capacity announcement is not a product test or a compatibility guarantee. Buyers comparing a new supply option would still need the relevant product documents rather than infer performance from the size of the manufacturing project.
