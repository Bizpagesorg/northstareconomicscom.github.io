Sveza prepares robot-assisted plywood handling in Tyumen

Sveza plans robot-assisted handling across several processes at its Tyumen plywood mill, with testing and installation preceding routine production.

2025-02-04

Sveza is preparing a broader use of industrial robots at its Tyumen plywood mill. The investment programme combines several material-handling operations, making the site a pilot for automation across production rather than a demonstration of a single machine.

A programme for the Tyumen mill

Interfax reported on February 4, 2025 that the group was investing around 400 million roubles in robotic systems at the plant. The company linked the project to a shortage of skilled workers. Installation was planned during 2025, so the announcement described an implementation programme, not a completed year's production result.

Tests focus on the way sheets move

In its announcement of the same date, Sveza said that more than twenty manipulators would cover four processes. Early testing with the project integrator examined machine settings, productivity, plywood stacking accuracy and the gripping of veneer sheets. The intended tasks included sorting, repairing material and feeding sheets into package assembly.

What the project brings together

  • Moving sheets between operations without treating every sheet as an identical handling problem.
  • Matching the robot's grip and placement to the material being processed.
  • Connecting individual handling tasks with the sequence of work at the mill.

These tasks explain why a plant-wide project involves more than purchasing manipulators. A robot can move a sheet successfully while the next operation is still waiting for space or material. The practical question is therefore whether adjacent stages can work together, not simply how quickly a machine performs one movement.

From a pilot to an operating process

The distinction matters for assessing progress. Equipment selection, testing and routine operation provide different kinds of evidence: a successful trial establishes a particular capability, while regular production shows how that capability fits the mill's working schedule. The announcement provides no basis for calculating a site-wide productivity gain or a reduction in total employment.

The useful next checkpoint is the transition from tested movements to connected production tasks. That keeps attention on implementation at Tyumen rather than assuming that a pilot automatically establishes the same operating result at every other mill.
