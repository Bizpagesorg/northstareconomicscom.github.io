Konar brings turbine casing machining further into its Chelyabinsk operation

Konar opens a Chelyabinsk turbine-casing line, extending machining before delivery. The announced output increase depends on reaching design capacity.

2026-04-03

Konar has opened a turbine-casing production line in Chelyabinsk, Interfax reported on April 3. Investment exceeded RUB 1 billion, including an RUB 800 million Industrial Development Fund loan. The project adds machining capability to the group's work on large components for power-generation equipment.

A different delivery boundary

In a company release carried on the website of group member BVK, Konar chief executive Valery Bondarenko described a shift from supplying castings or forgings with preliminary processing to delivering parts ready for assembly. The release says the project purchased 18 equipment units. A tenfold increase in output is described as the outcome after reaching design capacity, not a result already achieved on opening day.

The important distinction is the stage at which responsibility for the component passes to the customer. A partly processed blank leaves further work before assembly. A more extensively processed component changes the scope of the supply agreement, even when the buyer is still purchasing a part rather than a complete turbine.

More processing is not the same as more finished turbines

The new line concerns casing parts. It should not be confused with a turbine assembly plant or with a measured increase in electricity generation. A finished machine also depends on other components and on the customer's own production sequence. The supplier's announcement therefore describes one part of a wider manufacturing chain.

For an order using the expanded capability, the commercial description needs to make the delivery boundary explicit. Otherwise, a quotation for a blank and a quotation for a more finished part may appear comparable even though they leave different amounts of work with the customer.

  • State the processing condition in which the part will be delivered.
  • Identify which subsequent operations remain outside the order.
  • Agree the documents and acceptance arrangements for the supplied component.
  • Compare quotations for equivalent scopes, not solely their headline prices.

These are purchasing distinctions, not engineering instructions or a claim that a specific component has passed a customer's qualification process. The opening announcement does not establish the terms of an individual contract. Its significance is a broader manufacturing offer, while actual delivery performance remains a separate question for subsequent orders.
