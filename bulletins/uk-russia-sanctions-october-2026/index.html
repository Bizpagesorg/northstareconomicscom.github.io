Britain announces new Russia-related sanctions

Britain announces 23 individual and entity designations and eight ships; separate Sakhalin-2 LNG licences have contract and time restrictions.

2026-10-01

The United Kingdom announced a new package of sanctions related to Russia on 1 October, covering 23 individuals and entities and eight ships. Reuters reported the announcement as part of Britain's effort to restrict Russian revenues and target people accused of abuses and disinformation activities.

Ships and other designations are separate categories

The government's announcement divides the eight vessels into three ships it describes as belonging to a shadow fleet, two providing bunkering services and three Russian-owned ice-class vessels. Those groups total eight; they are not additional numbers on top of the announced ship count. The 23 individuals and entities form a separate category.

Britain said the package was intended to disrupt revenue and disinformation networks and address abuses against civilians and children in Ukraine. These descriptions state the government's grounds for the measures. The announcement should not be read as a report that a vessel has been intercepted or that a named cargo movement has already stopped.

  • Three vessels are described by Britain as shadow-fleet ships.
  • Two are identified as providing those ships with bunkering services.
  • Three are described as Russian-owned ice-class vessels.

Separate LNG licences have a limited scope

Alongside the package, Britain issued targeted licences concerning UK involvement in liquefied natural gas imports to Japan and South Korea from the Sakhalin-2 project. The published conditions concern supply contracts concluded before 17 June 2025 and imports between 1 January 2027 and 31 March 2028. The licensing provisions are distinct from the new ship specifications.

The official licence information for Japan states that the permission does not authorise new contracts or trade with designated persons. The corresponding South Korea publication describes the same project, contract-date and time restrictions. Neither notice is a general removal of Russian LNG restrictions.

The package announcement records new measures and a separate, conditional licensing scope. It does not establish the final commercial effect on each trade route. A list of ships and entities, a licence covering specified activity and evidence of actual cargo movements describe different things; the announced counts should therefore remain separate from any later estimate of their operational impact.
